# Geospatial Technologies for Precision Agriculture

**Project focus:** GIS and emerging technologies for data-driven farming

**Topics:** GIS · Remote sensing · NDVI · Drones · IoT · Databases · AI/ML · Robotics · Precision agriculture

## Overview

This project examined how geospatial and digital technologies can support precision agriculture across crop and livestock farming. The report reviewed the role of GIS, remote sensing, drones, sensor networks, databases, artificial intelligence, machine learning and robotics in monitoring agricultural conditions and improving farm decision-making.

## Project objectives

The report aimed to:

- Explain the principles and evolution of precision agriculture.
- Examine technologies such as GPS, GIS, remote sensing, variable-rate technology and data-management systems.
- Explore crop-farming applications including soil sampling, yield mapping, precision planting and variable-rate fertilisation.
- Explore livestock applications such as animal tracking, feed management and herd-health monitoring.
- Assess benefits, limitations and adoption barriers.
- Review real-world case studies.
- Discuss future trends involving AI, IoT and robotics.

## GIS and remote sensing

The study treated GIS and remote sensing as foundational technologies for monitoring crop health, soil properties and environmental conditions. It discussed the use of vegetation indices such as NDVI and the integration of satellite imagery with GIS for crop-management and irrigation decisions.

## Drone technology and farm asset mapping

The report reviewed the use of UAVs for:

- Detailed crop monitoring
- Early identification of crop stress
- Targeted application of farm inputs
- High-resolution farm-asset mapping
- Support for resource allocation and planning

## IoT and sensor networks

The project explored the value of connected sensors for monitoring soil and environmental conditions in near real time. It linked these data to applications such as precision irrigation, fertiliser management and early disease detection.

## Databases, AI and machine learning

The report considered how databases and cloud-connected platforms can help manage large agricultural datasets. It also reviewed AI and machine-learning applications including:

- Predictive analytics
- Crop-yield prediction
- Weed detection
- Plant-disease diagnosis
- Crop classification and monitoring

## Robotics and automation

The project discussed robotic systems and autonomous machinery for agricultural tasks, including examples of automated harvesting and precise fertiliser application.

## Case studies reviewed

The assessment included examples from:

- Bowles Farming Company, California, USA
- Fair Oaks Farms, Indiana, USA
- Winegrowers in Bordeaux, France
- Ceres Imaging, Australia and USA

These were used to illustrate different ways precision-agriculture technologies can be adopted in practice.

## Challenges considered

The report also examined barriers to adoption, including:

- Cost and accessibility
- Data-management requirements
- Technical expertise and training
- Connectivity and infrastructure
- Interoperability and standardisation
- Privacy and data ownership
- Regulatory and environmental considerations
- Resistance to technological change
- Ethical concerns

## Future directions

The study explored several future areas for precision agriculture, including AI and machine learning, IoT sensor networks, robotics and autonomous systems, controlled-environment agriculture, blockchain and traceability, and big-data/cloud analytics.
