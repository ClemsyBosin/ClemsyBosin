# History & Evolution of GIS

**Project focus:** The development of Geographic Information Systems from early mapping to modern WebGIS and emerging technologies

**Topics:** GIS history · CGIS · SYMAP · ArcInfo · WebGIS · Geodesign · IoT · AR · AI/ML

## Overview

This project reviewed major influences on the development of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) from early map-based spatial analysis through computerised GIS, commercial desktop software, WebGIS and emerging geospatial technologies.

The report organised GIS development into three broad phases:

1. The pioneer era
2. GIS from the 1980s to the 1990s
3. GIS in the present and future

## Pioneer era

The project discussed early examples of spatial analysis and mapping, including nineteenth-century disease mapping, before focusing on major developments in computer-based GIS.

Key milestones covered included:

- Roger Tomlinson and the Canadian Geographic Information System (CGIS) in the 1960s.
- Howard Fisher and the Harvard Laboratory for Computer Graphics.
- SYMAP and other early computer-mapping systems.
- Development of data models and digital cartographic systems.
- The establishment of Esri in 1969.

## Technology as a driver of GIS development

A central theme of the report was the relationship between GIS development and advances in computing. The project discussed how improvements in storage, processors, software, relational databases and raster-image processing expanded the range of GIS applications and made spatial analysis increasingly accessible.

## Commercial GIS and desktop mapping

The report identified the 1980s and 1990s as an important period of commercialisation and wider adoption. It highlighted:

- ARC/INFO as a major development in commercial GIS software.
- ArcView and the expansion of GUI-based desktop GIS.
- Growth in spatial-data management and analytical capabilities.
- Early web-based interactive mapping.
- The increasing role of global datasets and spatial-data infrastructure.

## WebGIS and modern GIS

The project examined the shift from desktop-focused GIS toward online, connected and collaborative geospatial systems. It highlighted WebGIS and the geoweb as important developments that made geographic information easier to access, analyse and share online.

The report also discussed geotagging, geodesign and the broader integration of GIS with scientific and digital technologies.

## Emerging directions

The project considered several technologies likely to influence future GIS development, including:

- Internet of Things (IoT)
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Augmented reality and mixed reality
- Advanced visualisation
- Real-time spatial data
- Smart-city, environmental and disaster-management applications

The report specifically explored augmented-reality use cases in areas such as utilities, architecture, navigation, construction, tourism and field operations.

## Key takeaway

The study's main argument was that GIS development has been closely linked to technological progress. As computing, networking, sensors and visualisation technologies evolve, GIS continues to expand from a specialist mapping system into a broader platform for spatial analysis, collaboration and decision-making.
